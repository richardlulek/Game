## About This Game  (Steam-body med BBCode)

[h2]Own the city, one building at a time[/h2]
Most tycoon games hand you a spreadsheet. [b]The Landlord[/b] hands you a city —
a living, low-poly metropolis you can watch breathe while you build a property
empire inside it. Start with a single flat and a mortgage. Finish as a listed
giant that half the skyline answers to.

[h2]A city that's actually alive[/h2]
Seven hand-built districts sweep from the harbour and the suburbs to downtown and
the glass towers of the Financial District. Traffic rolls, pedestrians wander,
lights flick on at dusk. Every building you own is marked on the map; every deal
you make reshapes the streets. Turn on the overlays to read the whole city at a
glance — vacancy, condition, yield.

[h2]An economy with real weight[/h2]
This is where the depth lives. Yield is real (net operating income over what you
actually paid), and so is everything under it:
[list]
[*]A proper [b]balance sheet[/b] — assets, equity, loan-to-value, a credit rating that moves your interest rate.
[*]A [b]central bank[/b] that sets the policy rate on a Taylor rule — hot markets and construction costs push inflation, and the rate follows.
[*]Bonds, lending, bank relationships and [b]green ESG loans[/b] that reward an energy-efficient portfolio.
[/list]
Refinance at the right moment and you soar. Over-leverage into a rate hike and the
whole thing can come down.

[h2]Rivals, auctions and the boardroom[/h2]
You're not alone. Named AI moguls chase the same blocks you do. Fight them at
[b]auctions[/b], corner a district's [b]market share[/b], and when buying isn't
enough, take them over: [b]negotiation, due diligence, hostile bids, partial
stakes and post-merger integration[/b] — a full M&A boardroom most property games
never touch.

[h2]From one flat to a listed empire[/h2]
Your company climbs six tiers — individual owner, small company, established firm,
regional group, national giant, and finally a [b]listed empire[/b] via IPO. Each
rung unlocks new tools and raises the stakes. Hire managers and a portfolio
director so your empire runs while you plan the next move.

[h2]Be the landlord[/h2]
Buy, renovate, and lease. Set rents, chase down vacancies, fit out single- or
multi-tenant space, and keep condition up before your best tenants walk. Let a
building rot and the numbers — and the map — will tell on you.

[h2]Play your way[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]The Inheritance[/b] — a story campaign that teaches the game as you live it.
[*][b]Sandbox[/b] — no win condition, fully customisable: starting cash, interest rate, rivals, calm mode.
[*][b]Scenarios & challenges[/b] — sharp win goals, from a quick start to becoming the hotel king, with rivals racing you to the target.
[/list]

[h2]Built to run on your machine[/h2]
A clean multi-window interface keeps deep systems organised, and graphics presets
(Low / Medium / High) let the living city scale from a laptop to a desktop.

## Early Access  (svaren på Valves sex EA-frågor)

**Why Early Access?**
> The Landlord is made by a one-person independent studio, and its heart is a
> living economy — rents, rates, rivals and risk all pushing on each other. The
> single best way to balance a system like that is to put it in players' hands.
> Early Access lets me tune the numbers, the pacing and the AI with real players
> instead of guessing alone.

**How long will it be in Early Access?**
> Roughly 6–12 months, depending on how much the community wants added before 1.0.

**How is the full version planned to differ from the Early Access version?**
> The full release aims to add more scenarios and events, a deeper mid- and
> late-game (richer M&A and rival behaviour), a stronger tutorial, more
> quality-of-life polish, and localisation. The goal is to broaden and deepen the
> game — not to lock existing features behind the 1.0 wall.

**What is the current state of the Early Access version?**
> Fully playable end to end. The complete core loop is in: buying, building,
> leasing, financing, the central-bank economy, rival moguls, M&A, the six-tier
> company journey, all seven districts, plus a story campaign, a sandbox and
> win-goal scenarios. It's a complete game already — Early Access is about
> balancing and expanding it.

**Will the game be priced differently during and after Early Access?**
> The price may rise modestly at the 1.0 release as content grows. Buying during
> Early Access will always be the best-value way in.

**How are you planning on involving the Community in your development process?**
> Through the Steam discussions and reviews. Balance, pacing and priorities will
> be shaped directly by player feedback — the systems that feel off are exactly
> what Early Access exists to fix.
